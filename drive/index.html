The navigation card

Maneuver arrow
The next thing you'll do. The bright branch is your way; the dim one is the road you're leaving.
Distance
Counts down to where the maneuver starts — the gore, where the lanes separate. Miles above 0.2 mi, then feet.
Exit number
Match it to the green tab on the overhead sign. A/B exits are different ramps.
“Then…”
A second step that follows quickly, like a ramp that splits again. Favor that side early.
Lane strip
One arrow per lane, left to right. Highlighted lanes are on your route.
Blue route line
On the map, it runs from your car into the lane you'll need and along the road you'll take.

Signs and lines

EXIT 51A
The exit-number tab, on the side the exit leaves from.
LEFT
A left exit. Move left, early — the left lane is the fastest.
EXIT ONLY
The lanes above it can't go straight. Going straight? Get out from under it.
Shared arrow
An option lane: it can exit or continue. Good when you're not sure yet.
Dashed
You may change lanes.
Wide dotted
The lane is about to end or exit.
Solid white
Don't cross — usually the last few hundred feet before a gore.
Yellow edge
The left edge of a one-way road. On a ramp, it's on your left.

North Texas specifics

  • Frontage roads. Most exits drop you onto a one-way frontage (access) road before the cross street — so the exit comes early, and you finish at a traffic light.
  • Texas U-turns. Under many bridges a U-turn lane takes you from one frontage road to the other without a light. It's how you recover from a missed exit.
  • I-35E and I-35W. I-35 splits at Denton (and again at Hillsboro): E to Dallas, W to Fort Worth. Read the letter on the shield.
  • TEXpress lanes. Toll lanes down the middle of highways like I-35W, I-35E and I-635. You enter and leave from the left.
  • Stacks. The High Five (US 75 × I-635) and Fort Worth's Mixmaster (I-35W × I-30) have ramps that split again. Watch the “then” line.

Five habits

  1. Half a mile. Be in a lane for your route half a mile before the maneuver.
  2. One lane at a time. Signal, check the mirror and over your shoulder, move, settle — then the next.
  3. Match the number. The exit on the sign should match the one on the navigation, and so should the distance.
  4. Make room by easing off. To merge behind a car, slow a little; don't race it.
  5. Missed it? Let it go. Never swerve across the gore or stop. The navigation will reroute.
