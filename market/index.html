How the quantum probability is worked out

          

            
Five independent kinds of evidence each give a probability that the stock is higher at the end of the horizon:

            
    
              
  1. Price-pattern model: a logistic regression re-learned on every run from ten years of prices of all the stocks you listed, looking at momentum, trend, volatility, distance from the high and strength against the S&P 500. It's tested walk-forward, so it's fitted on the past and scored on later data it never saw. Its spread is trusted only as far as that test earned.
    2. 
              
  2. Monte Carlo futures: 3,000 possible futures stitched from real month-long stretches of the stock's last three years, with its drift pulled halfway toward the long-run market average.
    3. 
              
  3. Company reports: revenue growth and whether it's speeding up, operating and free-cash-flow margins, debt and the price you pay per dollar of earnings. These come from the latest 10-Q and 10-K filings in SEC EDGAR, scored on their own and ranked against the other companies.
    4. 
              
  4. Company news: the last 30 days of headlines, scored with a finance-specific word list. Newer headlines count for more.
    5. 
              
  5. Market mood: the same, for news about the market and the economy as a whole.
    6. 
            

            
Each probability p becomes a qubit, √p|up⟩ + √(1−p)|down⟩. The qubits are superposed, weighted by trust, and the answer is read with the Born rule, P(up) = |⟨up|Ψ⟩|² / ⟨Ψ|Ψ⟩. When sources agree, their amplitudes interfere constructively. When they disagree, they cancel. Fidelity (⟨Ψ|Ψ⟩) measures that agreement. When the evidence is thin, the state is mixed with the fully uncertain one, which pulls the answer toward 50%. The range shows how far the answer moves if you leave out any one source.

            
This is quantum-inspired maths running on your own CPU. It is a careful way to combine evidence. It isn't a quantum computer, and it can't see the future.
